Bulky Item Disposal Guide

How to dispose of a mattress in Los Angeles, CA

Last checked Oct 2026. Every fact below links to an official source; confirm before acting.

Curbside pickup & scheduling

  • LA Sanitation & Environment (LASAN) picks up mattresses and box springs as bulky items; it is an unlimited curbside service at no additional charge[1].
  • Pickup happens on your regular trash day, and you must request it at least one business day before your normal collection[1].
  • Schedule online (LASAN service request form), in the MyLA311 app, or by calling the 24-hour Customer Care Center at 1-800-773-2489[1]. You can also dial 311 or (213) 473-3231[2].
  • Have an itemized list ready (for example, "one mattress, two chairs") so the right-sized truck is sent[1].
  • Mattresses and other bulky items do not go in the black trash bin[3].
  • Also accepted at the curb: furniture, toilets and sinks, carpet (rolled and tied), large wood or doors[1]. Not accepted: auto parts or oil, construction materials, commercial appliances, paints or chemicals, medicines or needles, fluorescent lights[1].

Mattress bag requirements

The LASAN bulky item page checked does not mention a bagging requirement for mattresses. Not verified.

Fees & limits

No additional charge and no item limit for curbside bulky pickup, per LASAN[1].

California mattress program & retailer take-back

California's Mattress Stewardship Program, overseen by CalRecycle and run by the Mattress Recycling Council (MRC), covers used mattresses, box springs and futons[4].

If a new mattress is delivered, California law requires the retailer to offer to take back your old one at no additional cost; delivery fees may still apply, the retailer has up to 30 days, and may refuse a mattress that poses a health or safety risk (as summarized by MRC's Bye Bye Mattress)[5].

Drop-off & recycling

  • City drop-off days: LASAN runs free bulky item drop-off on one Saturday a month, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Remaining 2026 dates: Nov 21 and Dec 12[1][6].
  • Locations: East Valley, 11050 Pendelton St., Sun Valley; West Valley, 8840 Vanalden Ave., Northridge; Harbor, 1400 N. Gaffey St., San Pedro; West LA, 2027 Stoner Ave.; Central LA, 2201 E. Washington Blvd.[1]
  • Mattresses, box springs, futons and headboards are on the accepted list. You must be present with your most current LADWP bill and a state driver's license or ID; one visit per event; vehicles up to a flat/stake-bed truck[6].
  • Bye Bye Mattress: California is an MRC program state, with no-cost drop-off at participating locations; use the locator on its California page and contact the site first[5][7].

Not yet verified

  • Eligibility for apartment buildings: the LASAN page is labeled for single-family residents; multi-unit rules were not checked.
  • Whether mattresses must be bagged or wrapped.
  • Electronics: the LASAN page lists e-waste as accepted but also defines bulky items as non-electronic; confirm with LASAN.
  • Whether California has a mattress landfill disposal ban (none found in sources checked).

Book a paid hauler

If city service doesn't fit (too many items, large building, timing), a private junk-removal or mattress-recycling hauler is the fallback. Ask that they recycle the mattress.
