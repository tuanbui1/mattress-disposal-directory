Bulky Item Disposal Guide

How to dispose of a mattress in Philadelphia, PA

Last checked Oct 2026. Every fact below links to an official source; confirm before acting.

Curbside pickup with regular trash

  • Mattresses and box springs are collected curbside as trash on your regular trash day; they count as compactible furniture (like sofas), limited to two items per week[1][6].
  • The separate Bulk Collection appointment program does not take mattresses; put a plastic-wrapped mattress out with regular trash instead[2][3]. The City says it will keep collecting up to two large items per household on regular trash days, including mattresses wrapped in plastic[2].
  • Eligibility: City collection covers inhabited properties with six units or fewer (plus qualifying condos and co-ops); larger buildings and some business types must use private collection[1][6].
  • Look up your trash day on the City's collection day page or call Philly311 (dial 3-1-1, or 215-686-8686)[1][10].

Set-out times

  • Outside Center City: set out between 7 p.m. the night before and 7 a.m. on collection day from April 1 to September 30, and between 5 p.m. the night before and 7 a.m. from October 1 to March 31[1].
  • Center City (Vine St. to Bainbridge St., Schuylkill River to Delaware River): 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. from April 1 to September 30, and 6 p.m. to 6 a.m. from October 1 to March 31[1].
  • Sources conflict on the season dates: the City's 2020 Streets Department regulations put the summer window at April 1 to October 31 and the winter window at November 1 to March 31[6]. In October, setting out no earlier than 7 p.m. (by 7 a.m.) outside Center City, or no earlier than 8 p.m. (by 6 a.m.) in Center City, meets both versions.
  • Trash set out at other times counts as litter under the Philadelphia Code[6].

Mattress bag requirements

For curbside pickup, mattresses and box springs must be bagged and sealed in plastic mattress bags[1]; the regulations require them to be fully encased in a sealed plastic bag[6]. The City's Bulk Collection site warns that unwrapped mattresses are not eligible for regular trash collection and are subject to fines[2]. The City pages checked do not mention a separate bed bug rule.

Fees & limits

  • The City pages checked do not list a fee for putting a bagged mattress out with regular trash[1].
  • Limit: two compactible large items (such as mattresses, box springs or sofas) per week per property[1][6].
  • Violating the sanitation regulations carries a fine of $100 to $300, and each day a violation continues can count separately[6]. The regulations list settlement amounts of $50 if paid within 10 days, rising to $75 and then $90 after reminder notices[6].
  • Trash set out over the limits is treated as litter and subject to penalties; for big cleanouts the regulations say to use a private hauler[6].

Bulk Collection appointments (not for mattresses)

  • Free appointment pickup for large items such as appliances (refrigerator doors removed), metal-frame furniture, large toys, flat-screen TVs, passenger car tires (rims removed, up to 4) and metal bathtubs[2][3].
  • Up to four items per appointment and one appointment per household per week. Open to residential households, including buildings with up to six units[2].
  • Schedule online at bulkcollection.phila.gov or through 311. Appointments are first-come, first-served and can be booked up to one month ahead; new dates open after midnight each day[2][3].
  • Set items at your normal collection point the evening before (no earlier than 5 p.m. in fall and winter, 7 p.m. in spring and summer), out by 7 a.m., and label them with the 4-letter pickup code from your confirmation email[2].
  • Furniture with a wooden frame goes out with regular trash, not bulk collection[2]. Note that the 2024 launch announcement listed "household furniture" generally as eligible[3]; the current program site limits it to metal-frame furniture[2].

Drop-off at a Sanitation Convenience Center

  • Philadelphia residents can drop off mattresses and box springs unwrapped at the City's six Sanitation Convenience Centers[4][1].
  • Hours: Monday to Saturday, 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.; closed on City holidays[4].
  • Bring proof that you live in Philadelphia. Regular household items can be dropped off once a day and oversized items once a week. Vehicles must weigh less than 6,000 pounds; commercial contractors and commercial loads are not allowed; no construction debris[4].
  • Also accepted: bulk and large metal items or appliances (two a day), up to four tires a day, up to six receptacles or 12 bags of trash, electronics, recycling and more[4].
  • Locations and phone numbers: Port Richmond, 3901 N Delaware Ave, 215-685-1358; West Philadelphia, 5100 Grays Ave, 215-685-2600; Strawberry Mansion, 2601 W Glenwood Ave, 215-685-3981; Southwest Philadelphia, 3033 S 63rd St, 215-685-4290; Northwest Philadelphia, 300 Domino Ln, 215-685-2502; Northeast Philadelphia, State Rd & Ashburner St, 215-685-8072[5].
  • Sources conflict on bagging here: the current City pages say mattresses don't need to be bagged at a convenience center[4][1], but the 2020 regulations list mattresses as accepted only fully contained in a sealed plastic bag[6]. Bagging it anyway works under both.

Illegal dumping

  • Dumping trash or debris on streets, public places or someone else's property is illegal, and each single large item, such as a mattress, counts as a separate offense[9]. Anyone who hires someone to remove waste can be held liable for their dumping[9].
  • The City says fines are now up to $2,000 per item for dumping without a vehicle and up to $5,000 per item, per instance when a vehicle is used; cleanup costs can be recovered, and the Philadelphia Parking Authority can impound vehicles caught dumping[8].
  • If you see dumping happening, call 911 and don't confront anyone. To report it afterward, call 311 or use the City's online form with the location, a description of the people and vehicle (make, model, color, plate) and what was dumped[7].

State disposal ban

No Pennsylvania mattress disposal ban was found in the official sources checked; not verified. Pennsylvania DEP tells residents to contact their trash hauler, or their county recycling coordinator, about disposing of bulky items[11].

Recycling & retailer take-back

Bye Bye Mattress: the Mattress Recycling Council's Bye Bye Mattress program currently runs only in California, Connecticut, Oregon and Rhode Island[12], so it does not operate in this state.
