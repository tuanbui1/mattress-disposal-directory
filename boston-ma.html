How to dispose of a mattress in Boston, MA

Last checked Oct 2026. Every fact below links to an official source; confirm before acting.

Curbside pickup & scheduling

  • Boston requires an appointment for every mattress and box spring; they are not collected with curbside trash[1]. Schedule online on the city's Mattress Recycling page, or contact 311 / 617-635-4500[1].
  • City pickup is only for buildings with six units or fewer; larger buildings must go through the property manager or a mattress recycling hauler[1][2].
  • Place it curbside by 6 a.m. on your appointment day, or after 5 p.m. the night before, where trash is normally collected — not on private property, not blocked, nothing on top, not crushed or cut[1].
  • Cancel before 12 p.m. the day before; report missed pickups to 311 within 24 hours[1].
  • Accepted: memory foam, box spring, innerspring, hybrid, latex. Not accepted: futons, toppers and similar (those may go out with curbside trash)[1].
  • Furniture can go out with normal curbside trash on collection day; appliances (fridges, TVs, ACs) must be requested through 311[3].

Mattress bag requirements

Soiled, dirty, or bed-bug mattresses must be bagged and fully sealed; bags are sold at hardware stores or online. Bagging is also suggested in bad weather[1].

Fees & limits

Limit: 10 items per calendar year[1]. The city page does not list a fee for eligible residents[1].

Massachusetts disposal ban

Massachusetts bans mattresses (including box springs and foundations) and textiles from disposal or transport for disposal, effective November 1, 2022[4]. Mattresses contaminated with mold, bodily fluids, insects, oil, or hazardous substances are excluded from the ban's definition[5]. Textiles covered include clothing, footwear, bedding, towels, curtains and fabric — even worn or stained items can go to textile recovery[5][6].

Drop-off & recycling

The city lists private recyclers for large buildings, e.g. HandUp Mattress Recycling (drop-off $35/item), Green Mattress (Brockton drop-off), Raw Material Recovery (Gardner drop-off), Tough Stuff Recycling ($25 drop-off / $75 pickup)[2]. No city-run mattress drop-off site was found on the city pages checked.

Bye Bye Mattress: the Mattress Recycling Council's Bye Bye Mattress program currently runs only in California, Connecticut, Oregon and Rhode Island[7], so it does not operate in this state.

Retailer take-back

The city advises asking your mattress seller whether they will take the old mattress for recycling at delivery[1]. Specific retailer programs are not verified here.

Not yet verified

  • Whether any fee applies to eligible residents (none listed on the city page).
  • Hauler prices are as listed by the city, not confirmed with the companies.

Book a paid hauler

If city service doesn't fit (too many items, large building, timing), a private junk-removal or mattress-recycling hauler is the fallback. Ask that they recycle the mattress.
